§3How to read it

CEO pay is total compensation reported on Form 990 Part VII: salary and bonus from the organization and its related organizations, plus deferred compensation and benefits (columns D, E and F). It is the figure the hospital's board approved and the hospital reported.

Charity care is Schedule H Part I line 7a: free or discounted care given under the hospital's own financial assistance policy, at the hospital's cost. It is not bad debt and not the Medicaid shortfall, which hospitals often add when they describe their community benefit publicly. The IRS form keeps them separate; so does this report.

Hospitals usually describe their charity care at charges, the list prices on their chargemaster, which run several times what the care costs to deliver. The IRS does not accept that figure. Line 7a must be reported at cost, using either the hospital’s cost accounting system or its own ratio of total expenses to total charges, and net of any payment received for the care. When a hospital’s press release gives a larger charity-care number than this report, that is almost always the difference.

The ratio is CEO pay divided by charity care. At 100 percent, one person was paid as much as the system gave to every patient who could not pay.

A nonprofit hospital pays no federal income tax and, in nearly every state, no property or sales tax. The exemption is granted on the premise that the hospital serves the community in ways a business would not. This report puts two of the hospital's own numbers beside each other and lets the reader decide whether the premise holds.
